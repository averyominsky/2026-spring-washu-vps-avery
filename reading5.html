The broken up graphics in the beginning of the documentary were striking, however I initially found the opening confusing. Brian Eno describes himself as “musically naive,” which I found compelling in how he approaches music. Eno talks about music as branches of color, which resonates with me as someone passionate in visual art. It is interesting the Eno compares making music to layers of painting. I am drawn to Eno’s interest in evolution theory and the idea of complexity arising out of simplicity. The metaphor of music as planting something and letting it flower reminds me of the reading “My Website Is a Shifting House” and the concept of growing a website gradually. Eno also talks shows with his band and enjoys creating experiences for people. I appreciated his discussion of music as a way of connecting to oneself. Eno comments about learning to trust himself musically. Trusting my instincts is something I am always working on in my own creative practice. Eno raises the question, why do we like music? I am drawn to his answer that music makes individuals feel part of a crowd and something largely collective. Additionally, it is interesting Eno emphasizes the importance of the human voice in music. This makes sense as a voice feels intimate and grounding. I appreciate how Eno challenges himself and asks questions on music that he has never heard before. He is curious and experimental. Finally, his comparison of listening to music to taking drugs highlights how powerful and transformative sound can be.
