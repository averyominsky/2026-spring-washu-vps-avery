Interdisciplinary Connections Interview

Interviewee

Jacob Ominsky – Math and CS student at Duke University

Interviewer

Avery Ominsky – Communication Design student at WashU

Context

My twin brother and I grew up sharing experiences and perspectives from different disciplines. I have always been an art student, exploring creativity, design, and visual storytelling, while my twin brother has always been deeply “mathy,” immersed in numbers, logic, and coding. But now, more than ever, I see how our worlds overlap. Math and computer science can build creative thinking, just as art can inspire logical problem-solving. This conversation explores that intersection.

1. Interdisciplinary Connections

How can CS benefit art and design?

Tech allows digital art, animation, 3D modeling, and virtual stuff. Math and code help artists bring ideas to life. Basically, technology expands what artists can do.

Have you ever used math or code to make art?

Yeah, I took a virtual animation class and a 3D design class at Duke. Math and coding were key to making it all work. Code is helpful in seeing patterns, which relate to art in visualizing ideas.

2. Creativity

Are there patterns in math that feel like design principles?

Definitely. Geometry, symmetry, proportion, math gives structure like the basis of design. Visualizing solutions are big in both CS and design. Prototyping and testing are universal.

Does math ever feel “artistic”?

I guess with geometry. Geometry is basically shapes and patterns, which is kinda like art. Picture Spider-Man calculating angles and trajectories to beat Doctor Strange, that’s basically applied math as art.

3. Future

What excites you about the future of math or computer science?

AI. It has huge potential to change lives. AI changes how we think, create, and problem-solve. AI is also up for interpretation.

How do you see math and art intersecting in the next decade?

There are no straight answers, just interpretation. Geometry, AI, and digital tools will keep building. AI is a space where math and creativity could intersect naturally.

Reflection

My twin brother’s world of math and programming overlaps with mine of creativity and art. From geometry to AI, logic and creativity feed off each other. Patterns, structure, and problem-solving in math can be just as imaginative as any artistic process, while art can inspire new ways of thinking about logic and design. Our conversation highlighted that creative and analytical thinking work together.
